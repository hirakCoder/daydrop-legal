Countdown Widget Not Updating on iPhone?

Updated September 2026 · 5 min read · iOS 18 & iOS 26

Your countdown said 42 days yesterday. It still says 42 days. The app itself shows the right number, but the widget on your Home Screen is frozen in the past.

This is one of the most common iPhone widget complaints, and the cause is almost never the app. It's how iOS schedules widget refreshes. Here's what's actually happening, and the seven fixes in the order worth trying them.

First: what iOS does on purpose

Widgets don't run continuously. Your iPhone doesn't keep every widget awake and recalculating — that would drain the battery. Instead, an app hands iOS a timeline: a set of pre-rendered future states, along with when each should appear. iOS then redraws the widget on its own schedule.

Two consequences surprise people:

  • Each widget gets a limited number of refreshes per day. iOS allocates them based on how often you actually look at the widget. A widget you never scroll past gets refreshed less often.
  • Widgets can't tick by the second. A Home Screen or Lock Screen widget is a snapshot, not a live view. Anything counting down in real time — seconds ticking, a progress ring filling — is a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, which is a different iOS feature entirely.

So a countdown widget showing whole days is expected to change once a day, around midnight — not the instant the date rolls over. A few hours of lag on the day boundary is normal. Days of lag is not.

The seven fixes, in order

FIX 1 — THE USUAL CULPRIT

Turn off Low Power Mode. Settings → Battery → Low Power Mode.

Low Power Mode cuts background activity, and widget refreshes are among the first things sacrificed. A widget can sit visibly stale for hours while it's on. If your phone hit 20% yesterday and you tapped the prompt, this is very likely your answer.

FIX 2

Turn on Background App Refresh. Settings → General → Background App Refresh.

Check both that the master toggle is on, and that it's enabled for the countdown app specifically. With it off, the app can't refresh its timeline until you open it manually.

FIX 3

Set Date & Time automatically. Settings → General → Date & Time → Set Automatically.

If you ever set the date manually — to test something, or to get past a trial — every countdown on the device does its math against the wrong "today". The same applies after travelling if the time zone didn't update.

FIX 4

Open the app once.

Launching the app gives it a chance to rebuild and hand iOS a fresh timeline immediately, rather than waiting for the system's next scheduled window. This fixes it more often than it has any right to.

FIX 5 — AFTER AN APP UPDATE

Remove the widget and add it again. Long-press the widget → Remove Widget, then long-press the Home Screen → + → add it back.

When an app updates, a widget can hold on to a timeline built by the previous version. Re-adding forces a clean rebuild. This is the single most effective fix if the widget broke right after an update.

FIX 6

Restart your iPhone.

All widgets on iOS are drawn by one shared system process. When that process gets wedged, every widget goes stale at once — which is the tell. If your weather and calendar widgets are also frozen, it was never the countdown app. A restart clears it.

FIX 7

Update the app, then re-add the widget. App Store → your profile → scroll to pending updates.

Genuine widget-refresh bugs do happen and get fixed. If you're several versions behind, update first — then repeat Fix 5.

Quick diagnostic: check whether your other widgets are also stale. If Weather and Calendar are stuck too, it's the system (Fixes 1, 2, 6). If only the countdown is stuck, it's that app's timeline (Fixes 4, 5, 7).

What about Lock Screen and StandBy widgets?

Same rules, same fixes — they're all built on the same widget system. Two extra notes:

  • Lock Screen widgets are attached to a specific Lock Screen. If you have several (one per Focus mode), you need to add the widget to each one, or it will look "missing" when a different Focus is active.
  • StandBy widgets only appear when the iPhone is charging, in landscape, and locked. If StandBy shows nothing, check Settings → StandBy is on.

When it's genuinely the app's fault

Being honest about this: sometimes it is. If a widget still shows the wrong day after Fixes 1 through 6, that's a real bug worth reporting — a poorly built widget can request refreshes too rarely, or crash while rendering and leave the last good snapshot on screen.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my countdown widget stuck on the wrong number of days?

iOS hasn't refreshed it yet. Every widget gets a limited daily refresh budget, throttled further by Low Power Mode or with Background App Refresh off. Opening the app once usually forces a rebuild.

How often do iPhone widgets update?

iOS decides, not the app. A widget showing whole days typically only needs to change once, around midnight. There's no setting to make widgets refresh more often.

Why doesn't my countdown tick down by the second?

Widgets are snapshots redrawn on a schedule, so they can't animate. Second-by-second countdowns run as a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island instead.

Does Low Power Mode stop widgets updating?

Yes — it's the most common cause of a stale widget, and the first thing to check.
